# Doc Factory — Operations Manual

Stop re-editing last month's letter. Hand over a document you already trust,
and it becomes a fill-in-the-blanks version of itself — your wording, locked,
so every copy after that is just filling in what changed.

Then two things happen that a template alone does not do. Every document gets
checked against what that kind of document has to carry. And nothing gets
saved or sent until you have read it and said yes.

Everything here is plain steps and there is no account to make. You do not
install anything to USE this from a chat app — your AI runs the checks in its
own sandbox. If you are running it from a coding tool on your own computer
instead, that tool needs **Node 22.6.0 or newer** installed, which is the one
requirement the kit has; the README's "Dependency floor" section says why and
what you lose without it.

## What you get

- **Templates made out of YOUR documents.** Not ours. The wording that gets
  locked in is the wording you already send — that is the part that took you
  years to get right.
- **A check on every document.** The kit knows what an engagement letter, a
  change order, a proposal, or a status update is supposed to carry, and it
  tells you what is missing. Including in the document you have been sending
  since 2019.
- **A note of where every part came from.** Each finished document can tell
  you which of your own documents each paragraph came out of, and which parts
  you typed today.
- **A locked look** — every copy comes out with the same typeface, spacing,
  and letterhead, so your documents always match.
- **A record that you approved it.** Nothing is a usable file until you click
  to accept it, and that click is saved with the date.

## The three things you'll do

### 1. Turn a document you already have into a template

Say: **"make a template from this"** and share your best example — a PDF, a
Word file, or just paste the text. Your real one, with the real client's name
still in it. Stripping the client-specific parts out is the whole job.

If you have two or three of the same kind of letter to different clients, send
them all. Comparing them is how the AI knows which sentences never change
without asking you twenty questions.

The AI keeps the wording that stays the same every time, and turns the parts
that change into labeled blanks — it calls them **fields**, the same word from
any paper form. Each field has a plain name like "client full name" or "next
step," so it is obvious what goes there.

**Then it tells you what your document is missing.** This is the part people
do not expect. It checks your letter against what that kind of document
usually carries and reports the gaps — no scope boundary, no termination
clause, an ask with no deadline on it. It will not tell you what your document
should say; that is yours, and anything with legal weight is a question for
your attorney. It tells you what is not in there, and asks whether you want to
add wording or have it keep flagging.

Whatever you decide sticks. Add wording and it gets locked in. Say "leave it,
keep reminding me" and it shows up on every document until you handle it. Say
"deliberately left out" and it never mentions it again.

### 2. Fill a template and get the document

Say: **"fill out the client update letter"** (or whichever template you want).

**If you have sent this client one before, it starts there.** "Last month you
told Denise the curbing would be set by August 20. Did that happen?" Then it
reuses everything that has not changed — your address, your phone, your
standard fee — and asks you only what is actually new. The fifth one of these
is four questions, not eighteen.

Two ways to answer:

- **Just talk to the AI.** It asks you for each blank, one at a time, in plain
  English. This works in any chat, with nothing else open.
- **Use the form page.** Open the form in your browser, type in the boxes,
  press **Fill the letter**.

### 3. Read it, then accept it

You get **one page** with your document on it, in your letterhead, exactly as
it will print — with a light PREVIEW watermark across it, because it is not
finished yet.

Under the document, three things:

- **What I checked** — every check that ran, and anything flagged. Flags are
  not errors; they are things worth your eyes before it goes out.
- **Where this wording came from** — which parts are your locked wording, out
  of which of your old documents, and which parts you typed today.
- **Your verification** — a box to tick and a button that reads *I validate
  this and I want it*. The button does nothing until you tick the box.

When you click it, the watermark disappears, and two downloads appear: **the
finished document** and **your receipt**. The receipt has the date and time,
what the document was built from, and anything you accepted a flag on.

Then two actions and you are done:

1. **Open the file you downloaded** — double-click, it opens in your browser.
2. Use your browser's **Print** option and choose **Save as PDF**.

On an iPhone or iPad: open the file in the **Files** app, tap **Share**, tap
**Print**, then pinch **outward** on the preview to turn it into a PDF you can
save or send.

## Why the accept button exists

Because it is your name on the document.

The click is a record that a person read this and accepted it before it was
ever a file that could be sent. It goes in your verification log with the
date, which document it was, what it was built from, and anything you accepted
with a flag on it. If anyone ever asks who approved what and when, that is the
answer, and it is sitting in your own folder.

It is also the reason this thing is safe to use. Nothing here sends anything.
Nothing here decides what is good enough. You do, every single time, on the
record.

**Change something and it has to be accepted again.** A receipt covers exactly
the version you clicked on and nothing else.

## When it says no

Sometimes it will stop and tell you it cannot do what you asked. That is
deliberate, and there are seven conditions that trigger it:

- **You have not given it one of your own documents.** It will not make one up
  and call it your template. A template built from its wording instead of yours
  is the generic letter you already have.
- **The document does not repeat enough to be worth locking.** It counts. If
  there are fewer than 150 words that stay the same, or more than 60% of the
  document changes each time, it tells you both numbers and says this is a
  document you write fresh each time. You can tell it to build one anyway, and
  it will mark the result as a thin template and flag every sentence it had to
  invent.
- **There are fewer than three fields.** That is stationery, not a useful
  template. It stops rather than adding a slower process to a ten-second job.
- **A real client's information is baked into the wording it was about to
  lock.** It shows you the exact line. That one matters: locked wording goes
  out on every future document to every other client.
- **A one-off promise is about to become a standing promise.** It quotes the
  free service, discount, turnaround, waiver, or guarantee and asks whether it
  applies to everyone, only sometimes, or should be removed.
- **The source appears to belong to another business.** It stops and asks you
  to establish that the wording is yours to reuse.
- **Something it needs is missing.** It will not guess at an amount or a date,
  and it will not write "TBD" into a document with your letterhead on it.

A tool that always gives you something is a tool that will eventually give you
something bad with your name on it.

## Your brand on every document

During setup we saved your business name, colors, and contact line. Every
finished document uses them for the letterhead. To change your look later,
update your brand notes (the `branding` folder) or replace your logo file, and
every future document picks it up. You do not redo each template.

## Growing your library

Every template lives in its own folder. As you turn more of your documents into
templates — an engagement letter, a proposal, a fee-increase notice — each one
joins your library, with its own checks and its own history.

**Finding and naming templates.** Each template folder has a plain name
(`engagement-letter`, `retainer-agreement`). To see what you have, ask: "list
my templates." To pick one: "fill out the engagement letter."

**Retiring or renaming.** To stop using a template, ask the AI to rename its
folder with an `_old` on the end — or just leave it, an unused template costs
nothing.

**Your history and your notes are safe.** When you get an updated version of
Doc Factory, your templates, your check decisions, your record of where wording
came from, your run history, and your verification log are all kept exactly as
they are. Only the kit's own instructions get replaced.

## Want to see it work first?

There are four complete worked examples in the `examples` folder — a
landscaper, an accounting firm, a mechanical contractor, and a title agency.
Each one shows a real-shaped document going in and what came out, including the
runs where the kit refused.

They are there to show you the flow. They are not a substitute for running your
own document through, and the setup will not let them be.

## One rule before anything goes out

When a document is headed to a client (or anywhere official), the AI writes the
draft and **you** review and send it. The verification click is that review,
written down. It is not permission for anything to be sent on your behalf, and
nothing here can send anything.
