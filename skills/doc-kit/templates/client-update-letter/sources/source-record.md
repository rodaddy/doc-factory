# Source record — client-update-letter (FALLBACK SKELETON)

**This template has no customer source, and that is the point.**

| What | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Source document | NONE — written by rTech as a starter skeleton |
| Supplied by | not applicable |
| Extracted on | not applicable |
| Locked prose words | **62**, measured |
| Refusal gate (`extract-template.md` Step 2.5, 150-word floor) | **FAILS** |

Every real template gets this file filled in properly, with one row per
document the customer supplied: what they called it, the date on it, who it
went to (initials or company only — never the full client record), and the date
it was extracted. If two versions of a sentence differed between their
examples, the row records which one was locked and why.

**The source documents themselves are never copied in here.** They contain a
real client's information. What is recorded is the wording that became locked
prose, plus the metadata. If the customer wants their originals kept, they keep
them where they already keep them.

## What provenance looks like on a real template

From the worked example in `examples/01-coastal-grade-client-update/`:

```
| Document                        | Date       | Went to                        | Extracted  |
| client update letter (best one) | 2025-10-09 | Sagamore Property Group        | 2026-08-12 |
| client update letter            | 2026-01-22 | Bay Shore Commons               | 2026-08-12 |
| client update letter            | 2026-04-06 | Hauppauge Industrial Realty     | 2026-08-12 |

Locked from: the 2025-10-09 letter. The other two were identical in the
locked sections; where the closing differed slightly, the 2025-10-09 wording
was kept at the owner's direction.
```

That record is what lets a finished document say *"the scope paragraph is your
locked wording L4, taken from the engagement letter you sent on March 3"* a
year later.
