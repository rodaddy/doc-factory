## Extracting the template

> Show me the last one of these you sent — the one you would be happy to send
> again tomorrow.

**Nick:** *(pastes three letters)*

> Three of the same document to different clients is the best possible input.
> I compared them line by line. Here is what I found:
>
> **Stays the same every time (312 words):** the opening sentence structure,
> all three section headings, the closing paragraph, the sign-off, and the
> sentence offering to talk it through.
>
> **Changes every time (about 40% of the letter):** the client, the job, the
> dates, what got done, what is next, and what you need from them.
>
> 312 words of repeatable wording is well clear of the line where a template
> is worth building. Building it.
>
> One thing I am not going to do quietly. This sentence appears in all three
> letters and it names a client:
>
> > "the drainage fall away from the building entrance that was causing the
> > ponding you saw in the spring"
>
> That is specific to Sagamore's courtyard. If I lock it, it goes out on every
> letter to every other client. Do you want that as a blank I ask you for each
> time, or should I drop it?

**Nick:** Blank. That's the "what we completed" part, it's different every job.

> Done. It is now `{{what was completed}}` and I will ask you for it each time.

## The gap report — the part Nick did not expect

> I ran your letter against the checks for a client status update. Five of
> seven are covered by your own wording. Two are not:
>
> **1. Your ask has no deadline.** "Please let us know at your convenience" is
> the sentence. That reads as polite, and it is why you chase people. In all
> three letters, the ask has no date on it.
>
> **2. Your ask is in section four.** When the thing you need is genuinely
> urgent — a decision that blocks your crew Monday — it is sitting below three
> paragraphs of status. The person skims the status and never reaches it.
>
> I am not telling you your letter is bad; it is a good letter and I locked its
> wording, not mine. I am telling you those two things are what is costing you
> the chase. Two options: I can add a dated-deadline blank to the ask, and I
> can watch every letter for an ask due inside a week and offer to move it to
> the top. Or leave both and I will stop mentioning it.

**Nick:** Do both. The moving-it-up thing especially — that's exactly what
happens, they read the first paragraph and that's it.

> Both are in. The deadline is now a required blank on the ask. The
> move-it-up check runs on every letter from now on and only speaks up when
> the deadline is inside seven days.
